# ASL 1 Curriculum Plan

2026-11-04

## Overview

The site teaches a one-semester beginner ASL course in 6 units, modeled on [Signing Naturally Units 1–6](https://www.dawnsign.com/?p=63474), the most widely used university ASL 1 curriculum. Every lesson keeps its full plan, but each one is split into a **vocabulary track** and a **sentence track**. Milestone 1 builds only the vocabulary track, because our recognizer classifies isolated signs. The sentence track is written now and switched on in Milestone 2, once we have a model for continuous signing.

- **Milestone 1 (now):** single signs, fingerspelling, and numbers. Each is checked by the isolated-sign recognizer against that lesson's sign list.
- **Milestone 2 (later):** phrases and sentences, question facial expressions, use of signing space, directional verbs, classifiers, and storytelling.

## Milestone scope

The rule: if a skill can be checked one sign at a time, it belongs to Milestone 1. If it depends on several signs together, on facial grammar, or on placement in space, it belongs to Milestone 2. Sentence content stays in every lesson plan, tagged **M2 – deferred**, so the lessons stay intact and need no rewrite later.

| Content type | Milestone | How it's practiced | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single vocabulary signs | M1 | Watch, then sign it to the camera | Checked against that lesson's sign list, with a cutoff to reject signs outside it |
| Fingerspelling letters | M1 | Sign a letter, then short names | Static letters first; J and Z involve motion |
| Numbers | M1 | Sign a number, counting drills | Handled in ranges: 1–15, 16–29, 30–66, 67–100 |
| Receptive drills (watch a video, pick the meaning) | M1 | Multiple choice | No model needed; covers phrases too |
| Deaf culture and history readings | M1 | Reading plus a quiz | No model needed |
| Phrases and sentences | M2 | Sign a full sentence | Needs continuous recognition |
| Facial grammar (wh-questions, yes/no questions, negation) | M2 | Sentence drills | Needs face tracking tied to the sentence |
| Signing space, pointing, directional verbs | M2 | Sentence drills | Needs spatial reasoning across signs |
| Classifiers, role shift, storytelling | M2 | Narratives | Hardest; last in the course |

In M1, receptive drills may still show sentences on video, since the student only watches and answers. Only *producing* sentences waits for M2.

## Course structure

There are 6 units over a 15-week semester, following the pacing at SJSU: Units 1–5 are core, Unit 6 is optional, and there are review checkpoints after Units 2, 4 and 5. Each unit holds 3–4 lessons.

| Unit | Title | Weeks | M1 vocabulary focus | M2 sentence focus (deferred) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introducing Oneself | 1–3 | Greetings, alphabet, numbers 1–15, shapes, colors | Naming yourself and others, "who?" questions |
| 2 | Exchanging Personal Information | 4–5 | Numbers 16–29, people and roles, hobbies | Confirming and correcting, yes/no questions, topic-comment order |
| — | Review A (Units 1–2) | 6 | Checkpoint | — |
| 3 | Discussing Living Situations | 7–8 | Numbers 30–66, home, places, transportation, pets | Directions, location commands, "how long?" |
| 4 | Talking About Family | 9–10 | Numbers 67–100, family members | Pronouns and possessives, comparing siblings |
| — | Review B (Units 3–4) | 11 | Checkpoint | — |
| 5 | Telling About Activities | 12–13 | Days and time, chores, everyday verbs, jobs | Sequencing, how often, directional verbs |
| 6 | Storytelling (optional) | 14 | Story vocabulary | Role shift, classifiers, short narrative |
| — | Final review (Units 1–5) | 15 | Final assessment | — |

### Lesson template

Every lesson uses the same five parts. Parts 1–3 and 5 run in M1. Part 4 is written now but stays hidden until M2.

1. **Learn:** a video for each sign, with the gloss, meaning, and handshape notes.
2. **Recognize (receptive):** watch a sign or short clip, then pick its meaning.
3. **Produce (expressive):** sign each word to the camera. Scoring uses only this lesson's sign list.
4. **Use it in a sentence (M2 – deferred):** the lesson's target phrases and grammar point.
5. **Culture note:** a short reading on Deaf culture or history, plus one quiz question.

## Recognition and assessment (M1)

M1 needs about 135 word signs, the 26 letters, and the numbers 1–100. Today's model covers about 11 of those signs. Its 50 labels are an ASL Citizen subset chosen for training, not for the lessons.

- **Per-lesson label sets:** each lesson ships its own allowed-sign list. The recognizer scores only those signs, and rejects anything below a confidence cutoff instead of forcing a guess. Review lessons combine the lists of the units they cover.
- **Retraining:** the vocabulary model must be retrained on the curriculum sign lists, starting with Units 1–2 (about 45 word signs).
- **Fingerspelling:** static letters can come from the [ASL Alphabet dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/grassknoted/asl-alphabet), which is image-based. J and Z need motion, so they need video or the sequence model.
- **Numbers:** 1–15 are mostly single handshapes. 16–19 and 67–98 involve movement (twisting, rocking), so they need video clips.
- **Assessment:** each unit ends with a receptive quiz (watch, pick the meaning) and an expressive check (sign each word to the camera). Review A, Review B and the final follow the SJSU pattern: Units 1–2, then 3–4, then a cumulative 1–5 quiz. The narrative assignment moves to M2.
